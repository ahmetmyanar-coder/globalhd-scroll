From the restoration of a registered mansion to a military headquarters, from an Olympic pool to a university laboratory. What these twelve works share is not their scale: in every one of them, somebody else sets the rules. A conservation board ruling, a military facility restriction, a public procurement specification.

Every building begins as a line.

A dimension decided on paper determines what will still be standing years later. The only thing that closes the distance between the line and the concrete is the same rigour applied at every stage: the calculation done correctly, the specification followed, the work left to those qualified for it.

Once a building is handed over, who built it and how becomes invisible. The only thing that remains visible is whether it still stands.

A site, a road. The layout settles. The plan stands up. Concrete and steel. The façade is clad. And the building stands.

Standard of work

To work on a registered building is to comply with a conservation board ruling. To work under public procurement is to comply with the letter of the specification.

In this work, when speed is allowed to override compliance, what follows is not a saving but a rebuild. Whether something was built correctly is understood not at handover but years afterwards.

Construction and Contracting

Turnkey construction for public and private clients, carried through from the tender stage to handover by a single contractor.

The lowest-impact building is the one that is reused.

The largest environmental decision in construction is whether to build anew at all. To demolish a building and put another in its place is to spend, along with it, every material and hour already invested in the first.

Reuse

The registered building was not demolished; it was kept standing.

In the mansion restoration carried out for the General Directorate of Cultural Heritage and Museums, the building was structurally strengthened, its stone and timber fabric repaired, its façade rehabilitated and its services renewed.

Discipline of claims

We publish no environmental claim that is not documented.

Zero-waste certification, carbon targets, energy performance and environmental management system certificates do not appear on this page. Not because that work is not done, but because it is not yet documented. An undocumented environmental claim cannot be defended in a tender file or in public.

This chapter carries only what can be verified from work already done. When the documents are complete they will be published here with their sources.
